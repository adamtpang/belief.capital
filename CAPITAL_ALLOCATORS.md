# Capital allocators: who holds the money, and who funds startups

Written 2026-10-29. Sizes are rough mid-2020s estimates from general knowledge,
not sourced figures. Categories overlap, so the sizes do not sum. Verify any
number before relying on it.

Most of the world's biggest allocators never invest in a startup directly. They
give money to venture funds, which then invest in startups. So there are two
rankings: who holds the most money, and who a founder can actually raise from.

## Ranking 1: biggest pools of capital on earth

| Rank | Type | Approx. size | Funds startups directly? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Asset managers** (BlackRock, Vanguard, Fidelity) | $100T+ managed in total | Rarely, only late stage |
| 2 | **Pension funds** (Japan's GPIF, Norway, CalPERS) | ~$55T to $60T | No. They fund VC funds as limited partners. |
| 3 | **Insurance companies** | ~$35T | No, except corporate venture arms |
| 4 | **Sovereign wealth funds** (Norway, ADIA, PIF, KIA) | ~$13T to $15T | Rarely, mega late rounds only |
| 5 | **Private equity** (Blackstone, KKR, Apollo) | ~$8T to $10T | No. They buy mature companies. |
| 6 | **Family offices** | ~$6T | Sometimes, and often flexible |
| 7 | **Hedge funds** (Citadel, Bridgewater, Tiger) | ~$4T to $5T | Some do late-stage rounds |
| 8 | **Venture capital** | ~$2T to $3T | **Yes, this is their whole job** |
| 9 | **University endowments** (Harvard, Yale) | ~$1T+ | No. They fund VC funds. |
| 10 | **Angel investors** | tens of billions a year | **Yes, the earliest money** |

Big tech corporations sit outside this table. The four largest planned about
$725B of capex in 2026 and run venture arms such as Google's GV.

## Ranking 2: who to raise from, in startup order

| Stage | Who | What you need first |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Pre-idea | Customers paying you | Nothing. Revenue is the only money with no strings. |
| 1. Idea | Friends, family, grants, hackathon prizes | A believable person |
| 2. Pre-seed | Angels and accelerators (Y Combinator invests $500k) | A working product and early users |
| 3. Seed | Seed funds (First Round, Conviction, South Park Commons) | Real usage or revenue growing every week |
| 4. Series A | Top VCs (Sequoia, Benchmark, a16z, Founders Fund, Accel) | Roughly $1M+ yearly revenue, or strong growth |
| 5. Growth | Thrive, Tiger, hedge funds, sovereign funds | A proven business that needs fuel |

## The best, by reputation and track record

- **Accelerator:** Y Combinator, by a wide margin. It backed Airbnb, Stripe and
  Coinbase.
- **Venture firms:** Sequoia, Benchmark, Founders Fund and Andreessen Horowitz
  are the usual top tier. Benchmark is small and returns-focused, and a16z is
  the largest with the most support services.
- **Angels:** operators who built companies in your space. Their introductions
  matter more than their checks.

## Where we stand on this ladder

Stage 0 to 1. No project has yet proven OFFER, REACH and CONVERT together
(see `MONEY_FORMULA.md` at the Aether root), and no investor above funds that.
Y Combinator is the realistic first target and is free to apply, but its
question is "how many users and how fast are they growing?"

The fastest way up the ladder is paying customers first. That proves the
business, raises the valuation, and sometimes removes the need to raise at all.
